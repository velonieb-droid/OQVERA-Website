What should a small business automate first?

Start with the moments where slow or forgotten responses cost money: an instant acknowledgment when an inquiry arrives, a polite follow-up a few days after a quotation, and a reminder before every appointment. These three automations cover most lost revenue — and none of them require automating your whole business.

FAQ

Questions business owners actually ask

Will automated messages sound robotic?

Not if they're written well. We write them the way you'd actually text a customer — short, warm, in your voice — and every sequence stops the moment the customer replies or books.

Can it automate Facebook Messenger replies?

Yes — instant acknowledgments and common-question replies can run on Messenger, with a clear handoff to a human for anything that needs one.

What shouldn't be automated?

Negotiation, complaints, and anything emotional. Automation handles timing and repetition; people handle judgment. We'll tell you plainly when something should stay manual.

Do customers need to consent to messages?

Yes — follow-ups relate to inquiries customers made, and marketing messages only go to people who've agreed. We build the consent step in, per the Data Privacy Act.

Can I start with just one automation?

Absolutely. Most clients start with instant inquiry replies plus quote follow-up. Add more when the first ones prove themselves.

Which follow-up is costing you the most right now?

Bring one real example to a free call — a quote that went quiet, a no-show — and we'll show you the automation that would have caught it.
